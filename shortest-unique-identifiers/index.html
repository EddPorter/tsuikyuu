Just enough letters

To pick one word out of a list you rarely need the whole word. This page finds the fewest letters that do the job, then lets you change the rules and watch how the answers, and the work, change.

The loop

For each word, try pieces of it, shortest first. For each piece, count how many words in the list would match it. Stop at the first piece that gives the answer you want. That is all the program does. Three of its lines can be swapped for others, and they are highlighted.


      

        
match.candidates
Which pieces of a word to try, and what it means for a piece to match another word.

        
counter
How to find the words that match a piece: ask every word, or look it up in a table built once.

        
goal.accept
What count makes a piece good enough: one word, at most two, or every word.

Type your own words, or pick a preset. Press Play to watch each word being tried, or Step to go one piece at a time. The three groups of buttons swap the three highlighted parts.

Things to try

    
    
      
  • Run the first preset with Contains and Only this word. banana needs three letters, but bandana and cabana need only one each.
    • 
      
  • Switch to Starts with. Identifiers now have to be prefixes, like short git hashes, and they get longer.
    • 
      
  • Switch to Letters in order. Gaps are allowed, the way a fuzzy finder matches, and answers get shorter.
    • 
      
  • Load Nested. Some words contain no piece that another word lacks, so they get no identifier at all.
    • 
      
  • Set the goal to Every word. Now the loop looks for the longest text all the words share. Load DNA to see a tie: two pieces are equally long, and each word keeps the first one it meets, so the answers differ.
    • 
    

  


  

    
What it costs

    
Both ways of counting give identical answers. They differ in the work they do. Scanning asks every word about every piece. The index spends effort up front, then answers each piece in one lookup. Load the Trees preset to see the index pull ahead. With only three short words it may not.

One more way to count, not built here, is a suffix array or suffix automaton. It does the work in roughly the total length of all the words, but it is harder to explain than a table.
